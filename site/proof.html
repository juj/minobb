Minimum-volume bounding boxes have three edge contacts

A proof of Conjecture 7.1 of J. Jylänki, An Exact Algorithm for Finding Minimum Oriented Bounding Boxes (2015), in the stronger form that holds for every local minimum of the volume — with interactive figures for each case of the argument.

Status: this proof was produced with AI assistance and checked numerically (the Hessian formula against finite differences, and several hundred random “two edges + four vertices” configurations). It has not been peer reviewed, but the theorem has been formalized and machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib (see lean/README.md and the guided reading in lean-walkthrough.html; the formal proof replaces the calculus on SO(3) by explicit polynomial curves and obtains the torque balance from three one-parameter motions). The figures below compute everything live from actual polyhedra, so they double as a sanity check.

1. Statement

Let K ⊂ ℝ³ be a convex polyhedron with non-empty interior. A box circumscribing K is determined by its orientation R ∈ SO(3): its axes are ui = R ei and each of its six faces is a supporting plane of K. An edge–face contact is a pair (f, e) where the box face f contains the whole polyhedron edge e. Two contacts are distinct if the faces or the edges differ; so a box face flush with a polygonal face of K counts at least three contacts, and a polyhedron edge lying along a box edge counts two.

Theorem. Let a box circumscribing K be a local minimum of the volume among all circumscribing boxes. Then it has at least three distinct edge–face contacts, and they do not all lie on one pair of opposite box faces (so at least two of them occur on adjacent faces).

Conjecture 7.1 is the special case of a global minimum. The theorem also answers the question raised at the end of the paper (whether the conjecture extends to all local minima), and rules out the configuration O’Rourke suspected might be a local minimum: a box flush with exactly two edges and touching four further vertices.

2. Setup

Write h(u) = maxv∈K v·u for the support function and w(u) = h(u) + h(−u) for the width. The volume of the box with orientation R is ∏ w(R ei); we work with its logarithm

F(R) = ∑i=1..3 log w(R ei).

Fix a locally minimal box R0, and use its frame as coordinates, so ui = ei; let wi be its side lengths. Each of the six box faces meets K in a contact set: a vertex, an edge, or a polygonal face of K. Let N be the number of edge–face contacts.

Let M be the set of orientations near R0 for which every box face still contains the contact set it contains at R0 — the boxes that “keep all current contacts”.

3. Step 1 — first-order condition: a torque balance

Near R0 the supporting vertices of each box face come from that face’s contact set, so F is the maximum of finitely many smooth functions Fσ, one for each choice σ of a vertex vi± in each of the six contact sets: Fσ(R) = ∑ log (vi+ − vi−)·R ei. Perturbing R = exp([ω]×) R0, the derivative of Fσ in direction ω is ω · ∑i ui × (vi+ − vi−) / wi.

At a local minimum of a maximum of smooth functions, the directional derivative maxσ dFσ(ω) is non-negative for every ω, which by the separating hyperplane theorem means 0 lies in the convex hull of the gradients. The gradient is a sum of six terms, each affine in one vertex, so that convex hull is obtained by letting each vertex range over the convex hull of its contact set. Hence:

Lemma 1. There are points Pi+, Pi− of K, lying in the contact sets of the faces +ui and −ui, such that with Di = Pi+ − Pi− ∑i ui × Di / wi = 0.

Now define the smooth function — the log-volume of the box “through the six fixed points” —

G(R) = ∑i log ( Di · R ei ).

Three facts carry the whole proof:

  • F ≥ G near R0: since Pi± ∈ K, the width in any direction u is at least Di·u.
  • F = G on M: while a box face still contains its original contact set, the point Pi± is still a support point of that face.
  • R0 is a critical point of G: that is exactly Lemma 1.

So if M contains a curve through R0 along which G strictly decreases, the box is not a local minimum. In Figure 1 below, F is the blue curve and G the dashed orange one: they coincide for motions inside M, and F develops an upward kink above G for motions that break an edge contact.

4. Step 2 — the Hessian of G is negative semidefinite

Let xi = Di / wi and let X be the 3×3 matrix with columns xi; its diagonal entries are 1. A direct computation gives ∑ ei × xi = (X32−X23, X13−X31, X21−X12), so

Lemma 1  ⇔  X is symmetric.

Write p = X12, q = X13, r = X23.

Lemma 2 (key bound). |p|, |q|, |r| ≤ 1.

Proof. The points P2± lie in the box, so |D2·e1| ≤ w1, i.e. |X12| ≤ w1/w2. Likewise |D1·e2| ≤ w2 gives |X21| ≤ w2/w1. By symmetry the two entries are the same number p, so |p| ≤ min(w1/w2, w2/w1) ≤ 1. The same holds for q and r. □

Expanding R = exp([ω]×) to second order, with R ei = ei + ω×ei + ½ ω×(ω×ei) + …, one finds G = G(R0) + ½ H(ω) + O(|ω|³) with

H(ω) = −2|ω|² + 2(pω1ω2 + qω1ω3 + rω2ω3) − (pω3−qω2)² − (rω1−pω3)² − (qω2−rω1)².
Lemma 3. H(ω) ≤ 0 for all ω, and H(ei) ≤ −2. Moreover H(ω) = 0 with ω ≠ 0 only in the diagonal case: the box is a cube, K contains two opposite corners of it, every Di is ± the space diagonal d joining them, and ω ∥ d.

Proof. By Lemma 2 and the inequality |ω1ω2| + |ω1ω3| + |ω2ω3| ≤ |ω|², the first two terms sum to at most 0 and the squares are subtracted. Equality forces |ω1| = |ω2| = |ω3| and p = sgn(ω1ω2), q = sgn(ω1ω3), r = sgn(ω2ω3). Then |p| = 1 forces w1 = w2 and the chords to span the full box in the transverse directions; with all three, the box is a cube, every Pi± is a corner, and xi = si·(s1,s2,s3) with si = sgn ωi, a vector parallel to ω. □

5. Step 3 — fewer than three contacts leaves a free curve in M

Suppose N ≤ 2. Then no box face is flush with a polygonal face of K, at most two box faces contain an edge, and each such face normal lies in the relative interior of that edge’s normal cone. So near R0 the set M is cut out exactly by the equations “face normal ⊥ edge direction”, one equation per contact. Going through the possibilities (click a row to load it in Figure 1):

(Two edges cannot lie in the same box face without a whole polygonal face lying in it. In the last row: u1×a is perpendicular to u1 and u2×b to u2, so if they are parallel both are multiples of u3.)

All rows except the fourth. The tangent ω0 can be chosen not parallel to any (±1,±1,±1), so H(ω0) < 0 by Lemma 3. Along a curve γ in M with that tangent,

F(γ(t)) = G(γ(t)) = F(R0) + ½ H(ω0) t² + o(t²) < F(R0)

for small t ≠ 0 (the second-order expansion along any curve is governed by H because R0 is a critical point of G). This contradicts local minimality.

Fourth row. M is a curve γ with a forced tangent ω0. If H(ω0) < 0 we conclude as before. Otherwise we are in the diagonal case of Lemma 3: all Di = ±d, so G(R) = ∑ log |d·R ei|. Since ∑ (d·R ei)² = |d|², the AM–GM inequality gives G(R) ≤ G(R0), with equality near R0 only if R is R0 followed by a rotation about d. But a rotation about d by a small angle θ ≠ 0 does not stay in M: the function θ ↦ a·u1(θ) is a trigonometric polynomial vanishing at 0, and it cannot vanish identically, because u1(θ) sweeps a non-planar cone around d (u1 is neither parallel nor perpendicular to d), which would force a = 0. So its zero at 0 is isolated, the points of γ other than R0 are not rotations about d, and there F = G < F(R0). Contradiction again.

Hence N ≥ 3 at every local minimum.

6. Step 4 — two contacts on adjacent faces

Suppose all contacts, however many, lie on one pair of opposite faces ±u1. The other four faces touch single vertices, so rotations about u1 stay in M, and H(e1) ≤ −2 < 0 gives the same contradiction. So contacts occur on at least two non-parallel faces, and non-parallel faces of a box are adjacent. ∎

7. Remarks

  • What makes it work. The torque balance makes X symmetric, and then containment of the contact points in the box bounds each off-diagonal entry by a width ratio and by its inverse. That one inequality turns the Hessian of the six-point box volume negative semidefinite.
  • Relation to O’Rourke’s theorem. Steps 3 and 4 reprove Theorem 2.1 of the paper (two adjacent faces flush with edges) without analysing the degree-six polynomial, and exclude the “two edges, four vertices” local minimum that the polynomial was needed for.
  • Consequence for the algorithm. Every local minimum — in particular the global one — is among the orientations with at least three edge–face contacts (categories A–D of the paper), which is the set the algorithm enumerates.
  • What it does not say. The argument shows a box with a free curve in M is never a local minimum. It does not decide which three-contact configurations are minima; that remains the job of the enumeration.
  • Surface area. Whether the same approach settles the minimum-surface-area question mentioned in the paper has not been examined here.
